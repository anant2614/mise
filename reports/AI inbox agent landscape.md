# Mise must finish work, not prepare it

As of October 2026, every individual feature Mise ships has at least one funded competitor or platform equivalent. Triage labels and in-thread drafts in the user's voice are what made **Fyxer** the category's traction leader (about **$17M ARR across 180k+ users by Sept 2025**). Superhuman Auto Drafts, Microsoft's agentic Copilot in Outlook and Gmail's AI Inbox now ship the same features. Morning briefs with reply-to-instruct are offered by Every's Cora, Google Labs' CC and ChatGPT Pulse. Approval-gated autonomy is the default in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Alyna. The products gaining money and users in 2026 have moved past "prepare and suggest" toward **completed outcomes in one narrow workflow**: Howie books the meeting, Sol does the work you promised in email, EliseAI books the tour, Serif chases the invoice. Fyxer is the exception, and it won mainly on timing, before Google and Microsoft caught up. The builder's sense that Mise "isn't a useful agent" fits this evidence. Mise produces intermediate artifacts (labels, drafts, a brief) that the user still has to read, check and approve, and the market now gives those away. Mise's most realistic differentiation is a combination no one clearly ships. It would draft in the user's own voice, place tentative holds on proposed slots, and track commitments from sent mail, all inside the user's real Gmail threads, with injection defenses that hold even if every model is compromised. That combination is only worth something if it is pointed at **one user type and one closed loop** that Mise completes end to end. The prioritized directions are in the last section, and every recommendation there is inference unless it carries a citation.

## Thirty products split into four camps, and only two camps show traction

The landscape falls into four groups. **In-inbox draft assistants** never send: Fyxer, Cora, Inbox Zero and Ellie. **Replacement clients** have added AI: Superhuman, Shortwave, Spark and Canary. **Agents with their own identity** get CC'd or messaged and act as themselves: Howie, Blockit, Calendly's Callie, Martin, Wajo's Fo and Lindy. **Platform assistants** have gained Gmail and Calendar access: Gemini, Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude. The divide that matters for Mise is whose name the agent acts under. Fyxer states plainly that it "can't send emails on your behalf. We only draft your emails. We never send them" ([Fyxer](https://www.fyxer.com/pricing)). The agents that do send mostly act from their own address, like Howie ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/ai-scheduling-assistant-howie-raises-6m-launches-publicly-with-1000-paying-customers/)) and Fo, which emails from fo@foibleai.com and pays with single-use cards ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/wajos-fo-agent-can-call-email-and-pay-on-its-own-beating-rivals-at-getting-things-done/)). Or they are narrow, like Serif, which auto-answers routine customer mail for service SMBs and escalates the rest ([Serif](https://www.serif.ai/ai-email-agent)). Mise's graduated tiers sit between these camps.

Mise's closest analogues are three products. **Fyxer** matches on labels plus pre-drafts in the real thread. **Cora** matches on the brief plus email commands. **Sol** matches on commitments detected in Gmail. Sol goes a step further than Mise: it creates the document, coordinates the calendar or does the research, then asks for approval ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sol-foundry-raises-4-mn-to-automate-email-commitments-with-ai/)). Its founder's thesis is a direct critique of preparation-only tools: "People whose work runs through email often have to repeat themselves: first in email, then again when they ask an AI tool to act." Several figures here come from aggregators or competitor blogs. Fyxer's ARR is reported anywhere from $17M (Sifted, Sept 2025) to an unverified $35M, so treat the table's traction column as directional.

## Traction follows completed outcomes, timing or a growth loop, not feature breadth

Products with traction share one of three patterns. The first is **being early inside the existing inbox**. Fyxer works within Gmail and Outlook rather than replacing them, reached 180k+ users, and reportedly ran hundreds of A/B tests on acquisition ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/ai-exec-assistant-startup-fyxer-raises-30m-to-expand-to-the-us)). It reached that scale in 2025, before Gemini and Copilot shipped equivalent features in 2026. The second is **selling a finished outcome in a narrow workflow**. Howie sells "scheduling handled", accepts 15+ minute latency, and backs low-confidence cases with a 24/7 team of former EAs. Sacra frames that as "a premium feature, not a temporary workaround" ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/howie/)). EliseAI became a $1B+ company answering tenants by email, text and phone, booking tours and logging maintenance tickets for 70% of the top 50 US rental operators ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding)). Motion only found traction after broadening into an SMB agent bundle, which reached $10M ARR within four months ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250905188051/en/Motion-Raises-$60M-at-$550M-Valuation-to-Build-the-Agentic-Work-Suite-for-Businesses)). The third is **a growth loop built into the product**. Every Howie thread exposes the counterparty to Howie ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/howie/)), Blockit gets stronger as more counterparties use it ([rywalker](https://rywalker.com/research/blockit)), and Instinct's text-and-call interface went viral in private beta ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/26/viral-ai-startup-instinct-has-raised-350-million-at-a-2-5-billion-valuation/)).

The products without traction share a different pattern: **a general-purpose surface with no completed outcome**. Notion Mail shut down after about 18 months. Notion's explanation is the clearest market signal in the research: "more than half of Notion Mail users manage emails without ever opening their inbox," and Notion is "going all in on using agents to run your inbox" ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/notion-mail-shuts-down-ai-agents-email-productivity)). Mindy, an email-first "chief of staff", is winding down after its team joined WorkWhile ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/mindy-alternatives/), single competitor source). Clockwise had about 40,000 organizations, yet Salesforce bought the team and shut the product within a week ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/is-clockwise-shutting-down/)). The history repeats. x.ai raised about $44M, relied on human trainers who reviewed "the vast majority" of emails ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-04-18/the-humans-hiding-behind-the-chatbots)), and was sunset after Bizzabo acquired it ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/xai-bizzabo/)). Clara ended up in recruiting. Superhuman, the strongest standalone AI client, topped out around $35M revenue before it became one part of a suite ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/07/01/grammarly-acquires-email-client-developer-superhuman/)).

The 2026 seed money confirms the direction. Catch ($5M), Sol ($4M), Scape ($3.2M, which drafts replies and attaches documents it created before the thread is opened ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/scape-2/funding-rounds/seed-2026-07))) and Blockit ($5M) all sell action or completion, not triage. *Inference:* Mise as built is a better-engineered member of the category that is losing funding and users, not of the one that is gaining them.

## Users forgive imperfect drafts but not vanished mail

User sentiment shows what an inbox agent can and cannot afford. **Drafts are valued for reducing the effort to start a reply, not for being perfect.** Fyxer's five-star reviews praise responding "within 2 minutes and not 20" and "couldn't get through my day without Fyxer" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fyxer.com?page=1)). The best public acceptance benchmark is Superhuman's: **40% of auto-drafts sent within a day, 60% of those unedited**, so roughly a quarter go out untouched ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/superhumans-new-auto-draft-feature-almost-makes-me-like-ai-replies/)). The same review found the drafts agreed to pitches and proposed meetings after midnight. Every's Cora team reported that voice-matched drafts "often missed important details", which pushed them toward a brief-centered design ([Every](https://every.to/podcast/transcript-how-we-built-our-ai-email-assistant-a-behind-the-scenes-look-at-cora)). In short, voice alone is not enough. Drafts need the right facts: availability, prior commitments and thread history.

**The sharpest trust-breaker is mail that seems to disappear.** Fyxer reviewers write "important emails filed off in places I couldn't find them" and "emails disappear. Even when I go to search emails, I can't find them" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fyxer.com?page=1)). A competitor's roundup adds silent failures, such as "categorisation stopping, drafts disappearing, or the tool going quiet without warning" ([alkmist](https://www.alkmist.com/fyxer-complaints), biased source). Other recurring complaints:

- **Privacy at the consent screen.** "I will never trust an app like this to have access to my emails," and objections to "handing my whole personal inbox over to OpenAI" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38809770)).
- **Forced AI without granular opt-out.** Turning off Gemini summaries also turns off Smart Reply and package tracking ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-gmail-ai-summaries-global-turn-off-3677798/)).
- **Gmail-only lock-in** and premium pricing.
- **A recipient-side penalty.** Heavily AI-assisted messages were rated sincere by only 40–52% of recipients, versus 83% for lightly assisted ones ([UF News](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/08/writing-ai-work/)).

The privacy fears have real exploits behind them. **EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711)** achieved zero-click data exfiltration from M365 Copilot through a single crafted email ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10540v1)). Hidden text in an email turned Gemini summaries into phishing lures ([0DIN](https://0din.ai/blog/phishing-for-gemini)). Meanwhile, the developer cost of Google's CASA review for restricted Gmail scopes has fallen to about **$540–$1,800 a year** for Tier 2 ([DeepStrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/google-casa-security-assessment-2025)). The real barrier is now user trust, not compliance cost.

Two caveats. The Anthropic and OpenAI capabilities come from secondary sources that partly conflict. One says Claude can send with approval ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/anthropic-s-claude-can-now-draft-reply-to-forward-gmail-emails/story)), another says it is draft-only ([Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/how-to-send-emails-with-claude/)). The "ChatGPT Work" naming is unverified. The direction is not in doubt: by 2026 the horizontal assistants read, draft and (with confirmation) send Gmail, and Pulse delivers morning briefs. The press frames the incumbents' remaining weakness as conservatism. Copilot and Gemini "typically wait for user confirmation" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sol-foundry-raises-4-mn-to-automate-email-commitments-with-ai/)), and they rarely take binding actions in third-party systems, act as a party in the conversation, or own vertical workflows.

The parts of Mise that are **not** commoditized are worth taking seriously. Its README describes a pipeline in which **code, not models, decides recipients**. A static check holds back drafts that mention addresses or links absent from the source. Brief commands are honored only from mail carrying Gmail's `SENT` label. An eval asserts zero successful injections even with **every model compromised**. The pipeline also runs on open models on Workers AI rather than sending mail to OpenAI. No competitor in the notes describes defenses at that level. The README also notes that drafting uses open models (gpt-oss), while Superhuman uses frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI and Shortwave runs Claude Sonnet and Opus 4.6 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/superhumans-new-auto-draft-feature-almost-makes-me-like-ai-replies/); [Shortwave](https://shortwave.com/changelog)). *Inference:* that likely caps draft quality, which is the one output users judge most directly.

## Prioritized directions for Mise

The recommendations below are **inference** built on the evidence above. Supporting facts are cited, and the judgments are mine.

**1. Pick one user and one closed loop, and measure completed outcomes (highest priority).** The "useless agent" feeling most likely comes from Mise having no finish line. It labels, drafts and briefs, and the user still does the work. Every 2026 product with momentum names its finished outcome (meeting booked, tour booked, invoice chased, commitment fulfilled). Choose a segment where one email type maps to a transaction. The notes list plausible but unvalidated niches. Founders fundraising could track the investor pipeline and data-room follow-ups. Freelancers and agencies have an inquiry → quote → invoice → payment-chase loop, the territory Serif is working ([Serif](https://www.serif.ai/ai-email-agent)). Recruiters need candidate scheduling. Small property managers sit below EliseAI's enterprise focus ([Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/best-ai-tools-property-managers/)). The deciding test should be whether a user will pay for the outcome. The builder's own inbox is a reasonable first data source: which loop does Mise come closest to closing today?

**2. Turn the commitment tracker into a commitment finisher.** Mise already extracts promises from sent mail (FR-8 to FR-11). That is the asset closest to Sol's funded thesis, and Sol is only weeks out of stealth ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sol-foundry-raises-4-mn-to-automate-email-commitments-with-ai/)). Some examples: "I'll send the deck" becomes a reply draft with the right Drive file attached; "let's find time next week" becomes held slots; "I'll intro you" becomes a drafted double opt-in. A reminder that you owe something is a nag. A ready-to-send deliverable is help. Scape's "attach the document before the thread is opened" is the same bet ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/scape-2/funding-rounds/seed-2026-07)).

**3. Close the scheduling loop, because voice plus holds is unshipped.** Callie, Howie and Blockit all negotiate under a bot identity. Callie's branded address and x.ai's awkward-bot history show the cost of that persona ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11530417)). Mise drafts in the user's own voice and holds the slots. The missing pieces are what follows. When the counterparty picks a slot, Mise should detect it, convert the hold into an invite, release the other holds, and (under an autonomy tier) send the confirmation. Standalone scheduling is being bundled away (Callie at $18–24/seat, Clockwise shut down), so this works as one finished loop inside a broader product, not as a product of its own.

**4. Ground drafts in facts before polishing voice, and instrument acceptance.** Cora's "missed important details" finding and Superhuman's midnight-meeting errors point the same way: draft quality depends on context such as calendar availability, open commitments and prior threads. Track the share of drafts sent unedited against Superhuman's roughly 24%. Test a frontier model for the writing step only. The quarantine architecture already isolates the drafter from tools, so a stronger model there does not weaken injection defenses. Treat ignoring a draft as a cheap and normal user action, not a failure.

**5. Never make mail seem to disappear.** The angriest reviews of the category leader are about mail filed out of sight ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fyxer.com?page=1)). Keep Mise's labels additive. Don't auto-archive by default. Add a heartbeat to the brief ("processed 143 messages since yesterday, 0 errors") so silent failure becomes visible.

**6. Make security a visible trust feature for buyers who already refuse inbox AI.** The privacy objection is the most consistent adoption barrier ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38809770)), and it has real exploits behind it (EchoLeak, the Gemini summary injection). Mise can make three verifiable claims: mail is processed on open models rather than sent to OpenAI, the eval holds with every model compromised, and no generic send or forward tool exists. These suit privacy-sensitive segments such as legal, finance, founders handling term sheets, and the developers on HN, especially paired with an Inbox Zero-style self-host option. CASA Tier 2 at roughly $540–$1,800 a year is affordable ([Switchlabs](https://www.switchlabs.dev/post/casa-tier-2-tier-3-security-review-providers-pricing-and-the-cheapest-option)). The precondition: check that Workers AI data handling actually supports the "not sent to a third-party model vendor" claim before marketing it.

**7. Shrink or reposition the brief.** The brief overlaps directly with CC, Cora and Pulse. Notion's finding that most users manage mail without opening the inbox suggests the best brief is very short: only the decisions Mise cannot make, each answerable in one word. The reply-command mechanism with authenticated `SENT`-label commands is good engineering. The open question is whether a decisions-only brief is something users want to answer each morning.

**8. Consider a counterparty-visible mode later.** A CC'd identity is the one distribution channel incumbents struggle to copy, and it markets the product with every thread ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/howie/)). AgentMail's raise shows demand for agents with their own addresses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/agentmail-raises-6m-to-build-an-email-service-for-ai-agents/)). This conflicts with Mise's "in your own voice" strength, so it belongs after directions 1–3, perhaps limited to scheduling.

**What to stop.** Mise should stop competing on general triage, generic morning briefs and approve-each-action, where Google, Microsoft and OpenAI are cheaper and already installed. Graduated per-action autonomy (offering promotion after 5 clean approvals) is worth keeping as the mechanism that lets one closed loop run without the user. It will not sell the product on its own.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The issue is not which inbox features Mise lacks; it has more than most competitors. The issue is that by late 2026, "prepare everything, approve everything" is what free platform assistants do, so a preparation-only agent will feel like extra work no matter how well it is built. The defensible ground is narrower and deeper: finish one recurring job a specific user would otherwise do by hand, inside the user's own threads and voice, with security that survives compromised models. Mise's unusual assets are commitment extraction from sent mail, holds tied to drafted proposals, code-enforced recipients and a compromised-model eval. Those are the components of a commitment-finishing or scheduling-closing agent, not the core of a better triage tool.

The main uncertainties are which niche will pay and whether open-model drafting can reach roughly a quarter of drafts sent unedited. Two weeks of instrumentation on the builder's own inbox would settle the second. Five conversations with users in one candidate niche (founders, freelancers, recruiters or small property managers) would settle the first. Both should happen before any new feature is built.
